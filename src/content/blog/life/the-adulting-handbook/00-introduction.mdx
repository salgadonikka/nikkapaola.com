You know what's funny? I had this shirt as a kid that said *"Prevention is better than cure."* I wore it like I understood it.

I didn't.

Growing up, I discovered a lot of things. Some fun, most of them not exactly health-conscious. Staying up until 4am, skipping meals, drinking through college, treating sleep like it was optional. I operated on two hours of sleep more times than I'd like to admit. I was always underweight, which I never took seriously because on the surface everything seemed fine. I'd think about that shirt sometimes. And then immediately forget about it.

Then my body started keeping score.

In 2011, I was diagnosed with Pulmonary Tuberculosis. I've been anemic for as long as I can remember, and consistently underweight on top of that, which doesn't exactly help. And in 2019, at 29 years old, I was diagnosed with Thyroid Cancer. Twenty-nine. I genuinely thought I had all the time in the world. And then suddenly I was in a hospital room rethinking every single choice that led me there.

Cancer has a way of making that shirt make sense again.

---

In the years since, I've been doing the work. Learning what I should have been doing all along. Building habits that actually protect me. Paying attention to my body in ways I never bothered to before. And along the way, I kept accumulating knowledge about tests I should've been getting, vaccines I'd missed, routines that matter, financial decisions I should've made earlier, and thinking: *someone else needs to know this.*

This series is me documenting everything I've learned. Not as a doctor,  (very much not), not as anyone in the health or finance industry. Just as a person who got the wake-up call early and wants to pass the notes along.

If even one person reads this and books a test they've been putting off, that's enough for me.

<Callout type="note" title="A note before we start">
Nothing in this series is medical or financial advice. I'm not a doctor, not a financial advisor, and not anywhere near either of those industries. Everything here is based on research, personal experience, and conversations with professionals I've consulted along the way. Always loop in your own doctor for anything health-related.
</Callout>
